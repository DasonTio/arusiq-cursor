Surfaces, not pages: the sitemap and the ten flows

Version 1.0 turned every requirement family into a menu item and produced a fourteen-item sidebar. This revision separates what the product must hold from what a person must navigate: thirteen rail slots across three roles, everything else reached as an object, a view or a panel. Revised again in 2.1: the rail is a desktop sidebar for HQ and the technician queue, and HQ has no mobile rail at all.

What changed, and why it was wrong

Revision

Version 1.0 was built by walking the requirement families in D5. UR-AIR exists, so “Air quality” became a page; UR-CAR exists, so “Carbon” became a page. That is traceability wearing an IA costume. Requirements describe what the system must know. Navigation describes where a person goes. They are not the same list.

The surface model

The core idea

A page existing is not the same as a page being navigable. Four different kinds of surface were sitting in one list in v1.0. Separating them is what emptied the sidebars without removing anything.

KindHow you reach itRuleExamples here
destinationThe navigation rail or tab bar A place you go with an intent. Five slots per role; each slot must earn itself against the others.C-1 Home · A-4 Accounts · T-2 Map
objectA list, a search result, a deep link in a notice Has one address and one component set, with role variants rather than a copy per application. Never in the rail.O-UNIT · O-ALERT · O-WO · O-CASE
viewA tab or segmented control inside a destination or object Shares the parent’s scope and period selector, so two views can never disagree about the same thing.C-4.2 Savings · O-WO.2 Checklist
panelA sheet over the screen that prompted it Every transaction. You never lose your place to perform one, and one panel serves all three roles under different policy.P-CONTROL · P-APPROVE · P-REQUEST
settingThe configuration room, behind the avatar Changes behaviour beyond the current screen. Versioned, audited, desktop only.X-1 Thresholds · X-2 Tariffs

The two grouping tests

Join

Same question, same moment

Two surfaces belong together when a person asks about them in one breath. “What did it use, what did it save, what did it emit” is one question with three answers — so Insights is one destination with three views and one period selector.

Split

A false comparison would be invited

Two surfaces must stay apart when adjacency implies arithmetic. Indoor CO₂ in ppm and emissions in kgCO₂e now sit a destination apart — ppm in the space, kgCO₂e in Insights. The separation is structural, not a warning label.

The four-tier language the flows assume

Severity is the product’s core vocabulary, so it is the vocabulary of these diagrams too.

Critical · act now

Failing now or about to. Every roll-up takes the worst status of its children.

Warning · plan action

Degrading. It still works, but it will not keep working.

Normal · nothing

Within the expected envelope for this unit, this room, these conditions.

No data · restore visibility

Offline, no sensor fitted, or too stale to trust. Never rendered as healthy.

2.1

Which merges survive at any width

Only the semantic ones, and they are not up for review when the screen gets wider: Insights as one measurement; air quality as a property of a space; one attention queue per role; the work order’s four views; payments and cases belonging to a customer; configuration separated from operations.

2.1

Which were slot-driven, and relax on desktop

Devices and Activity inside Fleet, Map inside Work, Preventive inside Service. They were folded to fit five tabs, so on a wide screen they stop being tabs you enter and become second-level items always visible under their section — the same IA, one less click.

Device strategy

Revised in 2.1

Version 2.0 gave every role a mobile rail. That was wrong for HQ, who work at a desk with two monitors, and only half right for technicians. The split is not by role — it is by task and place. Where the work happens decides which width it is designed for.

Client

Phone is the product

Full parity at 375 px, because the client checks comfort from a sofa and pays from a WhatsApp notice. Desktop is the same five destinations with more room for trends — nothing lives there that a phone cannot do.

Technician

Desk for the queue, phone at the unit

Planning — today, the week, SLA, load, route — is read at a branch desk or on a tablet before leaving. Execution is one-handed at the machine: checklist, measurements, photos, closure. Both are designed, neither is a reflow.

Admin / HQ

Desktop, with a deep link for the urgent

No bottom tab bar, no mobile console. The phone is reached from a notice and carries three actions — approve, acknowledge, dispatch — as panels over a single object. Everything else opens read-only and names the desktop.

What this means for the ten-day sprint

Build order

The surface model changes the order. Objects and panels are built once and reused by three roles, so they come before the dashboards that link to them — which is also why the estimate did not grow when the map got denser.

  1. Days 1–2 · Shell and spine. Sign in, router, rails, language switch, the asset hierarchy with status roll-up, and the system states from S-7. Nothing role-specific yet.
  2. Days 3–4 · The objects. O-UNIT, O-SPACE, O-ALERT and O-WO with their views. Three applications consume these, so every hour here is spent three times.
  3. Day 5 · The panels. P-CONTROL, P-REQUEST, P-ASSIGN, P-APPROVE. Building them as sheets now is what keeps the dashboards thin afterwards.
  4. Day 6 · Client rail. C-1, C-2, C-3, C-4 with the provenance library. The 60-second comprehension test runs here first.
  5. Day 7 · Technician. The queue at desktop width, the work-order views at 375 px. Two viewports, one day, because they are genuinely two products of one object.
  6. Day 8 · HQ console. A-1 with decisions first, A-2, A-3 — drawn once for 1440 with the two-level sidebar. The phone cut is UF-10 only: a notice, an object, a panel.
  7. Day 9 · The sensitive features. The ladder (A-4, O-CASE, C-5.1), carbon and MRV (A-5, O-PACK), device trust (O-DEVICE). Built last because they are the ones most likely to be cut down rather than cut out.
  8. Day 10 · The demonstration. Seeded data for the ten journeys, one deliberate failure in each of the command, payment and device paths, and a run-through against the ten-minute target.

Open questions and assumptions

Honest limits

Billing under Account is the riskiest call here

It is commercially the most important screen in the rent-to-own model, and I have given it three entrances rather than a rail slot: the notice deep link, the Home banner, and Account. Clean in a diagram, and exactly the sort of thing that tests badly. Watch it in the first prototype review; promoting it to a fifth client destination is a one-line change.

Two decisions still block UF-06

Who may flag a space health-sensitive — the flag now has an owner and a record in X-3 — and whether one or two approvers are required at each step. Both change the gate nodes and the P-APPROVE panel.

Which technician surfaces are truly field surfaces

I have put the queue on a desk and the job on a phone. If technicians in this market are dispatched by WhatsApp and never see a branch desk, the queue is a phone surface too and Work needs a denser mobile list. One question to a service supervisor settles it, and it is cheaper to ask than to build both.

Search may outrank the hierarchy at scale

For an office with hundreds of units, S-4 is likely the primary way in and Spaces the secondary one. If the prototype review confirms that, search gets a permanent field in the header rather than an icon.

The Figma file could not be read

The existing wireframes were not reachable from this session, so the map comes from D5, D6, D7 and the D8 review alone. Where the built prototype already names a screen differently, keep its name and map the ID onto it — the structure, not the label, is what this document fixes.

Insights assumes one period selector

Energy, savings and carbon share it. If a stakeholder wants carbon reported on a financial year while energy runs on a billing month, the three views stop being one destination and the grouping has to be revisited.
